The explainer

The ITI measures whether transparency in public infrastructure is actually working, not just promised.

Most accountability tools stop at publication. The Infrastructure Transparency Index goes further: it asks whether the information governments release is complete, usable, and connected to real oversight, then turns the answer into a single comparable score.

Transparency is easy to claim and hard to prove. A government can publish a portal, hold a launch, and still leave citizens unable to find out what a road cost, who built it, or whether it was finished.

The ITI exists to close that gap between the promise and the practice. It is a structured assessment of a country's infrastructure transparency, scored from 0 to 100, that a government, a donor, or a citizen can read in one number and then unpack dimension by dimension.

What it actually measures

The ITI does not score a single portal or a single project. It scores the whole system that produces, sustains, and uses infrastructure information across the project lifecycle: the laws behind it, the institutions that run it, the people who can access it, and the data itself.

Transparency on the ground: project information published where citizens can see and use it.

How the score is built

From hundreds of indicators to one number

The headline score is not a guess. It is built up from the ground in a fixed hierarchy, so any score can be traced back to the evidence behind it.

Indicators
hundreds of evidence checks
↓
Sub-variables
grouped checks
↓
Variables
themes within a dimension
↓
Four dimensions
each scored 0 to 100
↓
National ITI score
one comparable number

Each level rolls up into the next, so any score traces back to the evidence beneath it and means the same thing in every country.

That structure is what makes the ITI a benchmark rather than a report card. Two countries assessed years apart, in different regions, can still be placed on the same 0 to 100 scale, and a single country can watch its own score move as it reforms.

To be clear

What the ITI is, and what it is not.

The ITI is

A practical benchmark

A comparable, evidence-based measure of whether infrastructure transparency works in the real world, across four dimensions, on one 0 to 100 scale.

The ITI is not

A credit rating

It is not a formal investment-risk certification or a verdict on a government. It is evidence to inform reform and fiduciary-risk conversations, not to replace them.
