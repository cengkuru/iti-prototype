The audience hub

The ITI is built to be used, not just read.

The same score answers a different question for each reader. Find yours below, see what the ITI gives you, and what to do next.

🏛
Government
“How do we improve, and where do we start?”
What the ITI gives you
A diagnostic across four dimensions that shows exactly which systems are strong and which to strengthen first, with a baseline you can measure reform against.
What implementation asks of you
A focal team, access to records, and a commitment to publish. The assessment is run with you, not done to you, and the ITI is not a verdict on your government.
See the implementation pathway →
🧑
Citizen
“What does my government do with public money for infrastructure?”
What the ITI gives you
A plain-language read on whether the roads, schools, clinics, and water systems near you are published, explained, and open to question, and whether that is getting better over time.
What to do next
Look at your country's score and its weakest dimension. If citizen participation is low, that is your government being asked to open up, and your cue to demand it.

Civil society
“Where is transparency failing, and how do we prove it?”
What the ITI gives you
Comparable scores and dimension-level gaps you can cite in campaigns, oversight work, and submissions, grounded in a recognised method rather than anecdote.
Advocacy toolkit
Read your country score, then ask: which dimension is weakest, who is responsible for it, what reform would move it, and what has the government promised? Use the trend to hold those promises to account.
Read your country score →
💰
Donor or investor
“Is our funding going somewhere transparent and accountable?”
What the ITI gives you
A consistent measure of transparency and reform progress to inform fiduciary-risk conversations and support decisions. Evidence to inform, not a credit rating to replace your own due diligence.
What to do next
Read the assurance case: methodology, quality, limits, cost, and how the ITI complements PIMA, the World Bank IGA, and OECD tools. Then compare scores across your portfolio.
See the funder assurance case →
🌎
Regional body
“How do our countries compare, and where do we focus regional effort?”
What the ITI gives you
A common scale for peer learning across member states, so you can target capacity-building where the gaps are widest, on the same method rather than competing ones.
The regional offer
A shared regional benchmark, peer learning between countries, country-level reform plans, and a regional report to anchor a donor roundtable. Comparison for learning, not a league table.
See the shared benchmark →
📰
Journalist
“What is the real state of infrastructure transparency here?”
What the ITI gives you
Country scores, trends, and standout gaps that point to concrete, checkable stories, with a method you can cite and a baseline you can track over time.
Press pack
The story angle is the gap: find the dimension where a country scores worst, and report the distance between what was promised and what citizens can actually see. Cite the figure, its source, and its status, all listed in the evidence ledger.
Source-safe figures & ledger →
🔬
Researcher
“Can I cite this, and reproduce it?”
What the ITI gives you
A fixed, documented framework scored from indicators up to one national number, so any score is traceable to its evidence and comparable across countries and years.
For citation and reproducibility
Use the published methodology, the dimension-level data, and the version it was scored under. Methodology versioning, a citation format, and downloadable datasets are part of the live publication, not this prototype.
